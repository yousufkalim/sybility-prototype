Project definition is the first step of the cyberrisk management process.

This preparation step allows the cyberrisk manager to define what will be assessed, identify the people that will participate, and document important context.

It is a best practice to group resources together under the same project when there are similarities between them that would make it most efficient to manage their security under a single security plan. When doing this grouping for your project, consider the following attributes of the resources:

  1. Function or Purpose
  2. Criticality
  3. Target or Current Data Classification
  4. Ownership

Control assessment is the second step of the cyberrisk management process.

During this step, potential vulnerabilities in your security practices are identified.

Security controls that benefit the resources included in the scope of your project are often implemented and controlled elsewhere in the organization. These are known as common controls because they are common across many parts of the organization. You may optionally select to inherit information about common controls from various groups who have opted to share this information with consumers of their services.

Risk Analysis is the third step of the cyberrisk management process.

Risk is a measure of the extent to which an entity is threatened by a potential circumstance or event and is typically a function of the adverse impacts that would arise if the circumstance or event occurs and the likelihood of occurrence. Information security risks are those risks that arise from the loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or information systems and reflect the potential adverse impacts to organizational operations and assets, individuals, or other associated entities.

The objective of the Risk Analysis step of Risk Management is to produce a register of the risks that will be managed.

Security planning is the fourth and final step of the cyberrisk management process.

During this planning and approval step, risk treatment decisions are considered and selected for each risk statement added during risk analysis. Proposed risk treatment decisions should be reviewed with risk owners to ensure they are consistent with risk tolerance goals.

The end objective of the Security Planning step of Risk Management is to produce an approved information security plan.

Risk Treatment

Select the treatment that you reccommend for each risk that was added to the Risk Register in step 3.

Risk Transfer Examples:
  • Purchase cybersecurity insurance.
  • Pursue legal options (e.g., business associate agreement (BAA)).
  • Transfer risk to a vendor or service provider, ensuring the party can handle the risk.
Risk Avoidance Examples:
  • Change project scope.
  • Change project objectives.
  • Clarify requirements to eliminate ambiguities and misunderstandings.
  • Gain expertise to remove information security risks.
Risk Acceptance Examples:
  • The value to the business of accepting the risk outweighs the potential impact of the risk.
  • The acceptance of risk is a strategy like any other and should be documented and communicated.
  • Risk acceptance can be passive. In this case, the consequences are dealt with after the risk is realized.

Risk Mitigation: Technical, administrative, and physical controls implemented to reduce likelihood and/orminimize impact. Select from suggested mitigations or add your own.
